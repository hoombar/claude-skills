# Ben Blog Feedback Patterns

This file captures durable preferences learned from Ben's feedback on generated blog posts. Treat it as a living reference, but only update it when Ben explicitly approves a new pattern.

## Current Patterns

- Prefer concrete openings over abstract framing. A number, moment, decision, or failure is usually stronger than a topic sentence.
- Do not optimise only for minimalism. If a reader may be unfamiliar with the idea, comprehension matters more than strict concision.
- Keep Mermaid diagrams when they clarify a feedback loop, workflow, system boundary, or new mental model. Do not remove them just because the loop can also be written as bullets.
- Be careful with critic passes that over-prune structure. The critic should improve the article, not flatten useful scaffolding.
- When a sentence feels too generic, rewrite it toward practical consequence: what changed, what became easier, what tradeoff became visible, or what decision became possible.

## Sentence Feedback Workflow

When Ben provides disliked sentences after a post has been generated:

1. Rewrite the sentence or paragraph in Ben's voice.
2. Ask what feels wrong if the feedback is ambiguous: too generic, too polished, too vague, too technical, too flat, too much AI rhythm, or not enough practical consequence.
3. Extract a durable preference rule from the feedback.
4. Present the proposed rule to Ben before updating this file.
5. Only add the rule after explicit approval.

Keep feedback rules short, practical, and tied to observable writing behaviour. Avoid vague rules like "make it better" or "sound more human".
